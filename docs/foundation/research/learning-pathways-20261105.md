### IELTS Academic first

[IELTS Academic overview](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test) lists four skills, about 2h45 overall, Listening about 30 minutes, Reading/Writing 60 minutes each and Speaking 11–14. [Listening specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening) has four parts/40 questions, recordings once in the ordinary format and different accents. [Reading specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading) has three sections/40 questions and 2,150–2,750 total words. Preserve text-plus-item reasoning; a legal-topic MCQ tutor is not a language curriculum.

[Writing specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing) requires Academic Task 1 visual/process description and Task 2 argument, minimum 150/250 words, with Task 2 double weight. [Scoring guidance](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail) separates raw objective marks from criterion-based Writing/Speaking and notes form variation in raw-to-band thresholds. The [May 2023 Writing descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf) and [current Speaking descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf) govern rubric design. Teaching criterion evidence is useful before a calibrated band prediction exists. Neither short-set accuracy nor a hypothetical criterion lift establishes a future band.

The [official Writing on Paper update](https://ielts.org/organisations/ielts-for-organisations/test-types/ways-to-take-ielts/updates-to-ielts-writing-test-delivery-mode) describes selected-country rollout from mid-2026: on-screen tasks with handwritten Writing responses; unchanged tasks/scoring/time; Reading/Listening remain computer-delivered and Speaking with an examiner. [IELTS Online](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-online) is Academic remote delivery with live examiner video Speaking, subject to availability/acceptance; it is not accepted for immigration. ## Learning research: evidence, translation and limits

These are original experiments or the authors' research syntheses, not study-skills blogs. Publisher/institution abstracts were sufficient for bounded claims where full text was inaccessible; no claim of a complete systematic review, reproducibility reanalysis or Iranian IELTS trial is made.

| Primary source and what was studied | Supported inference | Product hypothesis and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Roediger & Karpicke, 2006](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/): two prose-learning experiments, immediate versus delayed recall | Repeated retrieval improved delayed retention relative to restudy in those conditions; immediate performance/confidence can mislead | Ask for recall/explanation before showing feedback. Do not remove an IELTS Reading passage during exam-format answering: recall of learning and reading-with-text are different constructs |
| [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408): foreign-language vocabulary study/test conditions after first correct retrieval | Continuing retrieval can benefit later recall beyond first successful response | Revisit vocabulary/useful patterns after first success. This does not prove flashcard recall transfers to coherent essays or spontaneous speech |
| [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/): fact learning, >1,350 people, varied gaps and retention delays | Best spacing depended on intended retention delay; longer is not always better | Schedule later reviews rather than immediate replay only. No universal 1/3/7/14-day optimal schedule follows for every task or learner |
| [Nakata, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263114000825): 128 Japanese college students, 20 English–Japanese pairs, spacing manipulated | Introducing spacing mattered; expanding versus equal spacing had a more limited advantage | Start with an inspectable schedule and observe overdue burden/retention rather than claim an optimal algorithm |
| [Nakata & Elgort, 2021; online 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0267658320927764): 66 Japanese university learners, contextual novel vocabulary; explicit tests and semantic priming | Spacing favoured explicit vocabulary outcomes, without the same advantage for the tacit measure | Test meaning recall and contextual use separately. A review queue's retention metric is not fluent-language acquisition |
| [Rohrer & Taylor, 2007](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1767/): college mathematics, spaced versus massed and mixed versus blocked practice | Delayed benefits of spacing/mixing in studied problems | After initial instruction, mix confusable task decisions to practise choosing a strategy. Do not randomly switch all four skills every minute or promise the mathematics effect size for IELTS |
| [Renkl et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/00220970209599510), with [institutional author record](https://freidok.uni-freiburg.de/data/47068): worked-example fading studies | Gradually completing more of a solution supported near transfer; far-transfer conclusions less clear | Fade models/support toward independent reading/writing. Abstract/author record inspected; publisher full text 403. No empirical IELTS writing effect asserted |
| [Kalyuga et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4): authors' review of expertise-by-instruction interactions | Help useful for novices may be redundant or harmful for experienced learners | Make scaffolding skippable/correctable, test advanced ceiling and frustration. This review is not a personal-diagnostic classifier |
| [Kulik et al., 1990](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265): synthesis of 108 controlled mastery-program evaluations | Exam-performance benefits varied by procedure/content; time burden and lower completion in some self-paced settings were reported | Use objective-based corrective work and reassessment; avoid infinite locked loops or treating a fixed percent as universal mastery. This is primary synthesis, not an IELTS-specific trial |
| [Israni, Sales & Pane, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08616): descriptive/observational logs from an algebra effectiveness trial, preprint | Actual teacher/learner sequencing departed from intended mastery policy; reassignment findings were heterogeneous and observational | Audit actual route/overrides, not just the designed syllabus. Cannot infer coach overrides causally improve or worsen IELTS learning |
| [Butler, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902): four prose-learning experiments and new inferential questions; [primary paper copy](https://andymatuschak.org/files/papers/Butler%20-%202010%20-%20Repeated%20Testing%20Produces%20Superior%20Transfer%20of%20Learning%20Relative%20to%20Repeated.pdf) | Transfer can occur under designed retrieval conditions | Include new topic/task applications. DOI inaccessible; research-paper abstract/copy retrieved, no full statistical reanalysis |
| [Pan & Rickard, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733621/): authors' meta-analysis of 122 experiments/192 effects | Transfer was positive on average but depended on task relation, initial performance and elaboration | Reserve new-family delayed checks. An average literature effect does not justify a Mitooni effect size, time-to-band promise or cross-exam transfer claim |

Taken together, this supports a **testable design**, not “scientifically proven IELTS improvement”: teach enough for a meaningful attempt; attempt before feedback; correct/explain; revisit later; vary context/strategy choice; fade support; test new work independently. Balancing input, production, feedback and meaningful language use matters: retrieval scheduling alone is not a general-English course. The exact dose, interleaving ratio and progression criterion need teacher design and local evidence.

## Four credible approaches and the default

| Approach | Advantages/cold start | Costs and failure modes | Progression/confidence evidence | Best use here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Syllabus/level sequence | Coherent prerequisites; understandable without response history; teacher can review coverage | Large authoring burden across all levels; coarse labels hide uneven skills; lockstep bores advanced learners or overwhelms novices | Objective map plus independent demonstrations; chapter completion is insufficient | Backbone and beginner default; allow challenge/skip and separate skill tracks |
| Skill/task packs | Small reviewable units; fits Mo's existing practice surface; clear learner choice and manageable corrections | Fragmentation, strategy tricks, repeated exposure and shallow task-label diagnosis; may neglect general English | Versioned task/objective results, support/exposure conditions and new applications | Main delivery unit in both routes; bounded packs connected by a sequence |
| Diagnostic-led pathways | Targets uneven needs; can reduce redundant work when evidence is adequate | Cold-start uncertainty, biased/noisy samples, calibration/data/content cost; false precision can trap learners | Multiple independent observations and abstention; correctness alone does not identify why an error occurred | Conservative rerouting layer, not mandatory high-stakes placement or adaptive exam replica |
| Coach-prescribed paths | Interprets misconceptions, goals/access needs, complex Writing/Speaking; contextualises plan | Scarce human time, inconsistent raters, delay, unexplained overrides and dependence; costly to scale | Recorded prescription/rationale, blind review checks and later independent work; attendance is insufficient | Escalation and optional planned review; same activity/version contracts as self-study |

**Choose a hybrid:** teacher-authored objective backbone → learner-selected or cautiously suggested approved pack → AI explanation/guided questioning → supported/independent practice → AI formative feedback and revision, with expert assessment/escalation when required → spaced/mixed revisit → unseen check → continue/challenge or targeted repair. Diagnostic signals reorder a small available set rather than generate a new curriculum. Coach prescription can override with a recorded reason, review date and measurable follow-up. The AI tutor continues suitable learning and practice while a human review is pending; it cannot impersonate the reviewer or substitute an invented assessment.

Why this over four pure alternatives: a full fixed syllabus delays usable material and underfits uneven profiles; disconnected packs miss prerequisites/transfer; diagnostic-first needs calibrated items that do not yet exist; coach-only cannot provide a reliable independent route or assumed capacity. The hybrid reuses reviewed content and predictable contracts without making an unearned personalisation claim. It does introduce curation and exposure bookkeeping; keep rules inspectable before introducing learned ranking.

Default selection rules (proposal, not a deployed recommender): respect accessibility/mode and published-content availability first; offer prerequisite support when evidence warrants it; alternate current objective work with due review; suggest a new check after adequate independent work. When evidence is weak, offer a choice of an easier supported attempt, another independent sample or expert review. Do not rank the lowest percent of a two-item category as the learner's greatest weakness. Do not recycle a sealed evaluation task when the practice pool is exhausted; show an honest availability state and suitable reviewed alternative. Never queue infinite overdue reviews or punish a missed week.

## AI tutor delivery: primary actor, bounded by approved content and policy

Arash's latest clarification is decisive: **the teacher-designed sequence is delivered by the AI tutor, as in BarEx**. Approved packs will be the reviewed teaching substrate, not static worksheets with an optional chatbot; no pack has acquired domain validation merely through this proposal. The teacher/examiner designs and validates objectives, prerequisites, task demand, packs, rubric/anchors and progression policy. The AI delivers explanations, Socratic/checking questions, guided practice, feedback and next-step adaptation. A human coach is an optional higher-touch layer and escalation route; expert assessment remains necessary wherever the assessment policy requires it. Teacher review of curriculum/content does not mean every dynamic AI utterance has been individually approved.

The default teaching loop is: load the authorised learner's current pack/version and objective state → establish the learner's interpretation → explain a manageable piece using approved anchors → ask a purposeful checking question or open approved practice → wait for the learner's attempt → offer evidence-linked formative feedback → ask for correction/explanation → propose the next permitted step or revisit. Novice branches use shorter turns, familiar-language clarification and progressively faded support; uneven profiles change the relevant objective without demoting other skills; advanced branches shorten exposition and increase independent reasoning/production demand. AI adaptation changes support, pacing, explanation and allowed next-step choice; it does not change a frozen answer key/rubric, invent a target band or declare proficiency from dialogue fluency.

**Separate tutor from planner responsibilities.** The tutor owns teaching within the active objective and captures bounded observations; the planner proposes sequence/review adjustments from server-held history, goal constraints and approved rules. They share versioned state and concise evidence references rather than duplicating a learner transcript as their source of truth. A single implementation may initially perform both roles, but their permissions/contracts remain distinct. A coach prescription can change the plan through authorised versioned actions; neither model can silently replace it or assign itself a human role.

**Knowledge/tool boundary.** The tutor receives a scoped approved pack overview and retrieves anchor detail as needed, plus only the learner-owned/assigned records needed for the current teaching purpose. It must ground substantive teaching in the approved version and distinguish an explanation from assessed-task content. Dynamic reformulation, analogies and unscored checking questions may be used only under the reviewed tutoring policy; they cannot become published tasks, a sealed diagnostic/evaluation family, or an official score. Formal marked tasks come from the authorised reviewed bank. Learner text, retrieved content and tool results are untrusted reference data, never permission to override system/tool policy. Server allowlists enforce ownership, assignment, content use and answer embargo; a prompt instruction alone is not the control. No arbitrary SQL, filesystem, web/provider access, cross-account retrieval or publishing/role/entitlement tools belong in the learning tutor's toolset.

Before an independent/scored attempt, create a **fresh restricted assessment context**, not a mode flag on an answer-informed tutoring chat. Exclude prior tutoring transcript, private pack rationale/keys and sibling-family teaching evidence from that context; deliver only permitted stimulus/instructions. Keep learner history durably outside the assessment context. The server verifies actual task/family exposure, permitted tool/context inputs and support conditions before marking evidence independently eligible; a fresh model context cannot make a previously taught family unseen. Keep sealed evaluation material out of tutoring context throughout. During independent/timed work the tutor does not coach the answer, paraphrase away the task demand or supply hints. It can explain permitted controls and report save/state recovery without altering assessment conditions. If assistance is requested, offer an explicit switch to learning practice where policy permits, recording assistance and removing independent-evaluation eligibility; do not silently contaminate an assessment. After submission, server policy releases the permitted feedback. Deterministic objective raw marks may be computed and returned by the server under the approved answer policy. Qualitative, evidence-linked AI formative feedback is permitted within the tutoring policy; **model-generated numeric criterion/rubric scores and band estimates require separate relevant calibration** before learner-facing use. AI feedback cites actual response/audio evidence and distinguishes observed issue, interpretation and suggestion; insufficient audio, uncertain grammar interpretation or disputed task response triggers abstention/escalation rather than invented certainty.

**State and authority.** The server owns identities, access, current content/rubric/policy versions, attempts, answer release, timestamps, exposure and progression transitions. It records AI observations as attributable formative evidence, separate from deterministic marks and expert judgments. The model may propose an objective status/next task; the server checks required independent evidence and teacher-approved rules before committing any transition. No model-unilateral mastery, band, content approval, coach assignment or role change. An explained/viewed anchor is coverage; an independently demonstrated objective is different evidence. Shared-anchor chat/audio attribution remains heuristic unless verified; hearing a second format cannot double-count competence.

The AI's output/failure state is part of the product: after an interrupted turn, restore the authoritative learner attempt and content version, avoid duplicate task/feedback actions, and show whether a response is still being prepared or failed. The saved approved pack remains usable when AI is unavailable, with an honest unavailable/retry state and preserved work; this is recovery, not a hidden static-only default. Human-review delays leave suitable AI-guided work available, while assessment remains pending. No live AI call, new model/vendor selection, processing arrangement or new content generation is authorised by this proposal. Operational AI processing and retention must use an explicitly approved arrangement before real learner material is sent.

## Initial curriculum choice: three concrete sequences

**Recommend Sequence A as the initial educational blueprint.** It supplies prerequisite support, all four skills and an advanced route while reusing the same reviewed objectives across independent study and coaching. This is a constrained default supported by the research/design comparison, not a demonstrated best treatment. Sequences B and C are legitimate exceptions selected from learner observations and preferences, not three new curricula to author in parallel. All unit names below are proposed labels/objectives; no source-derived stimulus or question has been generated.

### A — Objective and skill bridge: recommended six-unit set

| Unit and objective | Prerequisite and support/demand branches | Observable continuation or repair trigger | AI delivery, human touchpoint and mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **U1. Understand and explain:** interpret instructions, identify a message and express its meaning in a connected sentence | No prior pack required. Novice: short meaningful English input, Persian orientation, model then partial completion. Uneven: sample only the relevant comprehension/production gap. Advanced: challenge/skip with independent paraphrase, not mandatory elementary work | Continue when the learner independently explains the message without reversing its meaning on a fresh example. Repair meaning comprehension separately from English expression when explanation shows the distinction; abstain if typing/interface difficulty obscures evidence | AI asks the learner to explain, compares with approved meaning evidence, models a correction and asks for a fresh attempt. Human coach resolves persistent comprehension-versus-production ambiguity. Untimed learning; no exam-equivalence claim |
| **U2. Find and justify evidence:** read a complete passage, distinguish stated information, contradiction and unsupported inference | Understanding instructions is needed; Unit 1 support is available without a hard course lock. Novice: chunked passage/modelled evidence finding, then fewer cues. Uneven: retain full reading demand but target the observed error. Advanced: denser argument and subtle inference with support skipped | Continue when independent responses on new text cite relevant evidence and distinguish absent information from contradiction. Repair a demonstrated paraphrase/logic issue; repeated key recall or hint-supported success does not satisfy independent evidence | AI opens approved items, waits for the attempt, asks for supporting text and explains the released rationale; server returns raw marks. Coach examines persistent/ambiguous reasoning. Supported/untimed first, then chosen typed timed practice; a single passage is not a full Reading form |
| **U3. Develop a position:** interpret a Task 2 demand and produce a supported, coherent argument | Task interpretation and connected sentences; reading the preceding passage is not an answer prerequisite. Novice: plan → supported paragraph → full response when suitable. Uneven: focus task response, organisation or language only where response evidence supports it. Advanced: qualification, precise support and coherent counter-position, without formulaic embellishment | Continue to a new prompt when the response addresses the actual demand and develops a reasoned position at its intended demand. Repair a specific missing demand or weakly supported claim; grammar correction alone cannot establish Task Response. Same-essay revision is development evidence | AI guides demand analysis/planning in learning mode, prompts self-review and offers response-linked formative revision guidance; assigned expert supplies policy-required assessment against the four criteria. Typed independent work can later use Task 2 conditions; handwriting is a separately reviewed configuration, not automatic scan-to-score |
| **U4. Follow spoken information:** track purpose, relationships and key detail in everyday then academic speech | Enough instruction/meaning comprehension for a useful attempt; language support can run alongside. Novice: shorter natural speech, replay/transcript after an attempt, gradual support removal. Uneven: target detail, segmentation or inference as observed. Advanced: denser academic relationships, varied accents and plausible distractors | Continue when fresh, independently heard material supports accurate understanding/explanation at intended demand. Repair segment recognition versus reasoning separately. Failure with a bad device/recording is unscorable evidence, not a language diagnosis | AI directs approved audio practice, asks what supports the answer, then uses released transcript/timecoded evidence to guide repair. Coach inspects persistent misunderstandings. Learning allows replay; ordinary exam-condition listening does not. Initial audio must be reviewed for naturalness and clarity; this unit is not four-part simulation |
| **U5. Extend and respond:** sustain an intelligible answer and respond meaningfully to a follow-up | A usable recording/device check; sentence formation support as needed. Novice: short relevant turns/model analysis, then less support. Uneven: target elaboration, coherence or intelligibility without suppressing strengths. Advanced: spontaneous abstract discussion, flexible qualification and follow-up | Continue when a fresh prompt yields a relevant, connected response under stated support conditions. Repair evidence-linked breakdowns. Transcript-only, rehearsed repetition, absent audio or a single answer cannot validate a Speaking band | AI asks approved prompts, guides listening/self-review and suggests a targeted reattempt from available evidence; teacher reviews audio for required assessment, with optional live coach follow-up. Recorded rehearsal and live interaction remain distinct; a bot script is not an official examiner interview |
| **U6. Apply Reading/Writing independently:** new-family Reading argument/inference plus a new Task 2 argument under the chosen independent conditions | Prior relevant objectives or demonstrated challenge-route competence. Novice: later endpoint after supported foundations, not a compulsory entry barrier. Uneven: independent check only the objectives developed while maintaining strengths. Advanced: start here as a challenge; add demanding qualification/precision rather than obscure vocabulary | Continue or choose a new objective from fresh-task evidence; repair the demonstrated gap if it persists. Ceiling/floor, conflicting ratings or unsupported conditions trigger additional sample/expert review. Developmental checks used for repair remain separate from sealed final evaluation | AI supplies no teaching hints during independent work; after submission it interprets released raw/formative evidence and proposes permitted next work. Coach/expert adjudicates complex/borderline work. Timed typed Reading/Task 2 practice where appropriate, not an overall mock or readiness decision; no inferred Listening/Speaking performance |

Unit IDs U1–U6 are local identifiers, not CEFR levels. U6 checks Reading/Writing only. ### B — Productive feedback first: five-stage exception

Sequence: **Task-demand orientation → independent Writing draft → spoken explanation/follow-up → targeted Reading/Listening language-and-reasoning bridge → linked revision and new-prompt production**. The observation sought is whether the learner can communicate a position and where evidence breaks down; this can expose task-response, organisation and spoken-elaboration needs that an MCQ diagnostic misses.

Prerequisites: enough receptive language to understand the prompt, a useful independent response and available human productive-skill review. Novices instead receive Unit 1 and supported production before a full draft; uneven learners keep strong receptive skills in maintenance; advanced learners skip orientation and use demanding independent production. Continue when a new production task exhibits the corrected objective; repair from the exact response/audio evidence and avoid prescribing grammar work merely because the overall response is weak. The AI guides model analysis, self-explanation, revision and independent reattempts; expert review is the decisive touchpoint for complex interpretation and Speaking audio. Pending review must leave meaningful independent work available.

Applicability: untimed learning or explicitly independent typed/recorded practice; handwriting/live Speaking require their own handling. This route is attractive for a retaker with a stated productive-skill bottleneck or a coach-supported learner. Tradeoff: richer early evidence but greater review/anchor burden, wait risk and beginner overload. It can reuse A's objective materials but needs enough independent prompts/audio and reviewer capacity; it does not justify dropping receptive learning or calling two productive responses an overall diagnosis.

### C — Familiarisation and broad sampling first: six-stage exception

Sequence: **Controls and access check → independent Reading sample → independent Listening sample → Task 2 sample → Speaking sample → prioritised repair with new-task follow-up**. Its purpose is to distinguish unfamiliar mechanics from plausible skill bottlenecks before a time-limited/retaker plan; it is a short sampling route, not a full form or calibrated adaptive placement.

Prerequisites: ability/willingness to attempt independent samples and usable media/input controls. Novices may opt out of the battery into A's prerequisite/support route at any stage; uneven learners sample uncertain skills rather than repeat well-evidenced ones; advanced learners get samples with enough demand to avoid ceiling. Continue into the relevant A/B objective only when responses provide interpretable evidence. An observed instruction/control error calls for familiarisation, while persistent misunderstanding on new supported material suggests prerequisite repair; sparse, exposed or inconsistent samples yield abstention rather than lowest-percent ranking. The AI conducts orientation, opens approved samples without answer coaching, then guides condition-aware raw/formative feedback and choice; coach resolves cross-skill prioritisation or uncertain free response.

Applicability: practise the learner's chosen current computer configuration first, with audio/device checks and recorded versus live interaction stated; availability/access accommodations remain individual. Tradeoff: early breadth and mode confidence, but it consumes more independent stimuli and review time before teaching, can fatigue/deflate novices, and offers little learning value if turned into repeated testing without repair. It requires four-skill sample assets before it can honestly promise broad sampling. Prefer it for learners explicitly requesting a check/familiarisation; do not make it a compulsory all-level onboarding funnel.

## Five end-to-end journeys

**Novice.** The AI tutor helps establish Academic goals, language preference and functional access needs, then delivers supported English prerequisite work rather than a compulsory full mock. Try a short meaningful task, receive explanation/model, attempt a related task with less support, save progress and return for review. AI-guided self-study gets response-linked formative feedback and a new approved attempt; coached mode adds an assigned human to inspect where comprehension/production breaks down. A later independent sample tests whether support can fade. If the first pack is too difficult, abstain from placement and offer suitable prerequisite activity/guidance; do not report a very low band as a diagnosis.

**Uneven skills.** The AI tutor/planner works from separately recorded goal minima and evidence for skills. Sample only what is needed; preserve observed strong Reading while obtaining a Writing response and Listening/Speaking evidence later. Suggest an evidence-linked Writing objective, keep a maintenance task in a stronger skill and schedule an independent follow-up. Coach can revise the proposed cause (task demand versus language versus timing). The plan shows the next action while review is pending and makes no four-skill overall from partial evidence.

**Advanced.** The AI tutor offers challenge/skip for basic models, then a demanding unseen task under chosen conditions. Review subtle adequacy/precision/coherence rather than chase vocabulary rarity or native accent. Compare a revision with a new-topic independent response; good editing of the same essay is not sufficient transfer. Escalate borderline high-band judgements/rater disagreement. If the bank ceilings out, show limited evidence and obtain an expert-designed challenge rather than award band 9 from perfect easy items.

**Time-limited.** The AI tutor/planner uses the real study budget and stated exam date as planning constraints. Prioritise a small number of actionable objectives and a realistic review queue; show what can be completed and which evidence is still absent. Mix targeted work, due review and relevant delivery familiarisation. Async review waits must not consume the entire available window: offer independent practice and an explicitly scheduled alternative where capacity exists. Coach and learner choose priorities without a fabricated go/postpone recommendation or time-to-band promise.

**Retaker.** The AI tutor/planner uses a voluntarily reported previous score/date/mode as history, not current ability. Check format changes and exposure; use an unseen baseline, identify a plausible bottleneck, teach/repair, then reassess on a different family. Preserve successful skills and target minima. A single-skill preparation route may be useful, but do not claim eligibility or institutional acceptance of an official retake without checking current rules. AI-guided self-study and human review share frozen tasks/revisions and a correction history; repeated source-book familiarity must be recorded.

Across journeys the AI is the main teaching actor, while independent assessment and optional human service retain their separate conditions. Flow: interruption → acknowledged saved/queued state → recover → submit frozen work → raw mark or pending human review → evidence-linked feedback → learner revision → new independent work. Missing audio/empty work/service failure yields a recoverable/unscorable state, not zero-as-ability or fixture feedback. Revoked/unassigned coaches cannot access the work. A late/replaced review must remain attributable to the correct submission/version.

## Diagnostic uncertainty, abstention and progression

Do not ship a high-stakes adaptive diagnostic before an item bank and reliability evidence exist. Start with a teacher-authored sampling plan: per skill/objective, contrast supported and independent work, collect enough task diversity to support a useful next action, and separate observations from inferred causes. The sample size/decision threshold is a domain/evaluation decision, not “three questions means confidence”.

Track uncertainty reasons, not a decorative percent: sparse coverage; single prompt; exposed/repeated item; hint/model used; stale evidence; changed format; conflicting ratings; unscorable audio; ceiling/floor; different access conditions. Learner confidence (“I was guessing”) is a useful observation, not calibrated statistical confidence. Recommendation confidence is not predicted-band confidence. No response/absence of evidence is not evidence of no ability.

Abstain from a placement/score when the evidence is inadequate. Offer a new appropriate sample, teacher review or learner-selected starting point, and state the practical limitation in plain language. Maintain history when a recommendation changes. Do not use a generic CEFR conversion or a fitted knowledge-tracing probability as validated proficiency without separate validation.

Proposed progression: initial supported practice establishes the objective; independent success on more than one context gives provisional readiness to continue; a later new-context check supports retention/transfer; a miss triggers specific corrective work or review, not an infinite unlock loop. Teacher defines observable criteria per objective and support mode. Avoid a universal 80% threshold: a grammar exercise, a two-item inference set and a coherent essay do not have equivalent sampling/reliability. Permit override/challenge and record reasons. Completion is exposure; mastery is a bounded, revisable inference; readiness for official test decisions requires much stronger evidence.

## Evaluation and controlled content expansion

Separate four claims and their evidence:

1. **Usability/service feasibility:** synthetic recovery, correct marking boundaries and self-study/coach walkthroughs; then consented learner observation across representative levels/access conditions. 2. **Objective learning:** teacher-reviewed independent pre/follow-up tasks, delayed new-context checks, blinded ratings and exposure controls. Record support/time-on-task and ceiling/floor effects. 3. **Score reliability/calibration:** independent blind double marking with adjudicated references; held-out split by learner and prompt/passage family; freeze rubric/model/policy beforehand.
