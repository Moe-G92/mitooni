## What exists at the reviewed revision

These are source observations, not claims of production exploitation. The repository is an interactive browser prototype. `web/src/main.tsx:11–15` enables MSW unless `VITE_USE_MOCKS` is exactly `false`, with unhandled requests bypassed. `web/src/api/client.ts` sends cookie credentials to `VITE_API_BASE` or the same origin; it casts responses rather than enforcing the Zod contract. No production server/database or server authorization enforcement was found in the inspected tree. Setting the flag does not create a secure backend.

- **Identity is simulated.** `web/src/mocks/auth-handlers.ts` accepts any password, accepts any six-digit OTP, and supplies a canned Google identity. `web/src/mocks/browser.ts` gives those handlers precedence over older routes. `web/src/app/RequireAuth.tsx` gates navigation through `/me`, not data authorization. Lookup returns account existence and name. The contract recognises enumeration risk but does not implement rate limits (`docs/handoff/api.md`).
- **All identities share one local database.** `web/src/mocks/db.ts` uses `bw-db` in localStorage for profile, attempts, results, messages, accounts and vocabulary; failed persistence silently continues in memory. Sign-out flips `signedIn` (`web/src/mocks/handlers.ts:60–64`); query cache is cleared (`web/src/api/queries.ts:72`), but persisted attempts/messages/blobs remain. This is unsafe as a model for shared-device isolation.
- **Ownership and roles are absent from the contract.** `web/src/api/schemas.ts` has profile ID, `plan: standard|coach`, learner-facing review messages and a single coach. Attempts/results/reviews/slots have no learner-owner or assigned-coach relationship. A subscription plan is not a security role. `handlers.ts:123–174,225–246,260–263,325–393` retrieves or mutates objects by supplied IDs without per-object authorization; several routes do not even check mock sign-in. `/me` broadly spreads a client patch, including plan/ID fields. These handlers must not be transplanted into a server.
- **Essays and voice are sensitive content.** Essays, notes and quoted feedback sit in the attempt/result structures; speaking feedback adds a transcript. `handlers.ts:225–246` puts uploaded blobs in IndexedDB under a generated key and returns audio by key, without ownership, actual duration/type/size verification or cleanup. Deleting an attempt only removes its attempt record (`150–154`), not recordings/results. Upload even accepts a blob for a nonexistent attempt and returns a key. Schema assertions in handlers do not validate hostile input.
- **Resume has limited evidence.** `web/src/exam/engine/session.tsx:147–185` debounces in-memory patches, restores a failed patch to an in-memory ref, and flushes on unmount. No durable network outbox, mutation ID, revision/concurrency control, cross-device merge or explicit successful acknowledgment is present. `flush()` catches errors and resolves, so `web/src/pages/exam/ExamPage.tsx:110–113` may submit after a failed save. Submits increment section index and can recreate result/notification effects (`handlers.ts:195–222`) without idempotency. The contract says the future server must own timing; the mock trusts client state.
- **Audio can be lost before upload.** `web/src/exam/sections/SpeakingRoom.tsx:395–413,575–595` holds takes in React state/refs and joins/uploads each part only during submit. Reload/kill before upload loses these takes; partial upload retry may create duplicate/orphan blobs. A visible retry error exists, but no durable upload journal. `media.ts` correctly requests audio only, not video; recorder formats include ogg although the handoff lists webm/mp4. Microphone lifetime and track stopping need a reproduced navigation test, not assumption from a helper's existence.
- **Answer hiding is presentation-level in this build.** `web/src/mocks/tests.ts:151–173` strips item answers/explanations/evidence from the returned object, but spreads part audio including transcript; fixture/test/scoring modules still execute in the browser. `web/src/mocks/mocks.test.ts:47` checks stripped answers, not bundle or transcript leakage. Public `/audio` and `/figures` assets are directly downloadable. That is acceptable for a controlled demo, not proof of exam-key or licensed-pack protection.

## Threat model and minimum boundaries

Assets: identity/contact, learner essay and voice, inferred level/accommodations, coach messages, assessment integrity, licensed/source resources, derived original packs and feedback, availability and cost budget. Actors: another learner; unassigned/former coach; compromised coach/admin account; shared-device user; automated scraper/OTP abuser; malicious uploaded content or prompt-injection text; network interruption; processor/operator mistake. Do not assume essays or speech contain only learning content: names, migration plans and sensitive personal stories can appear.

Updated authority: Arash selects adaptation and reuse of the existing BarEx infrastructure, code, assets, services, integrations, hosting and AI as the baseline; payments remain unresolved. Persian is the default with an English option; all learner levels/targets and teacher/examiner review are confirmed. No greenfield vendor selection is proposed. Preserve Mo’s frontend and adapt the smallest suitable BarEx components. The security boundary remains a first-party application/API with explicit access policy and private Mitooni learner storage, plus an asynchronous assessment adapter. Self-study and human coaching must remain usable when automated marking is unavailable. Shared provider infrastructure/accounts may be reused where permitted; this does not require sharing learner identities, content, secrets or authorization.

1. **Server authority.** Authenticate every data route; derive learner ID from session, never request body. Use secure, HttpOnly session cookies with appropriate SameSite/CSRF and origin checks, short privileged sessions and coach/admin MFA. Separate learner, coach and operational permissions. Entitlements and accommodation approval policy are server controlled; allowlist editable profile fields. The API and database must both enforce ownership where supported; service credentials never enter browser code.
2. **Coach isolation.** Store `learner_id`, `assigned_coach_id`, review ID, selected immutable submission version, assignment/revocation history and status transitions. A learner explicitly shares the selected essay/recording for review. A coach receives only assigned reviews and necessary scheduling/profile data; no global learner search, contact exports or whole-account access. Reassignment immediately revokes old access, including new download authorization. Audited exceptional admin access needs a reason, bounded duration and named human owner. Do not encode an author role supplied by the client.
3. **Private content.** Keep recordings in a private object store, keyed internally to owner/attempt/part/submission version. API-authorized streaming or short-lived scoped download URLs; keys are identifiers, never authorization. Reject unauthenticated/range/HEAD downloads as appropriate; prevent public caching/referrer exposure. Quarantine uploads, validate magic bytes and decoded format/duration as well as byte limits, bound processing time, avoid user filenames as paths, and isolate transcoding. Store no raw payloads in logs, analytics, error telemetry or notification text.
4. **Minimise and erase.** Use a display name and one recovery channel where feasible; target/test date can support a plan without passport, travel documents, destination or political detail. Do not collect biometric identity or use voice identification. Define separate retention periods for raw takes, submitted recording/essay, transcript, derived feedback, coach thread, audit and backups before collecting real data. Erase linked content and queued/provider copies on deletion; retain only an explicitly justified minimal record. The current root instruction forbids raw customer data leaving this Mac: no cloud learner processing may begin until Arash explicitly establishes the permitted production data boundary. Synthetic fixtures remain the only test data now.
5. **Recoverable attempts.** A versioned IndexedDB outbox can preserve each acknowledged local draft/take, scoped to the authenticated account and with explicit shared-device behavior. Do not call this device encryption against XSS: script on the origin can access it. Use unique mutation/upload IDs, base revisions, transactional ordered saves, checksum-verified resumable uploads and exactly-once visible submit semantics. Server owns deadlines and score versions. Submission waits for server-acknowledged required content; incomplete or failed audio has an explicit incomplete state. Reconnect recovers without duplicating bookings, messages, marks or charges. No indefinite automatic retries after logout or permission revocation.
6. **Assessment/content boundary.** Keep answer keys/rubrics/source originals and marking credentials on the server; exam DTOs omit transcript/evidence as required by the exam mode. Practice reveals only authorised item feedback. Treat essay/transcript as untrusted data, use bounded scoring tasks and schema-validated output, no tool/browser execution or data access on learner instructions. Quoted malicious HTML must render as text. Provider calls carry pseudonymous job IDs and the minimum selected text/audio, never profile/contact or unrelated coach history.
7. **Abuse and operations.** Bound OTP sends/verifications by identity and network signals without locking out a whole carrier NAT; expiry, single use and replay protection; generic lookup/reset responses. Enforce per-user upload/storage/job/concurrency limits and system budget ceilings, moderation/reporting with human escalation, session revocation and restricted coach exports. Audit metadata with access policy, no essay/audio body logging. Build/deploy must fail if production enables mocks, demo identities or `/dev/reset`. Scan output bundles and network destinations. A third-party outage produces queued/unavailable feedback, never fabricated success or a fixture band.

Arash's confirmed full commercial/distribution resource rights for an Iranian audience are accepted. Do not reopen local resource-analysis permission. Preserve an asset ledger: source ID/path and hash, rights authority/date, territory Iran, allowed uses, transformation/pack lineage, derivative version, review/publication state and takedown relationship. Originals stay separate from learner submissions and published packs. This authority does not by itself establish permission to send originals to external models, train models, distribute outside Iran or use another project's restricted material. Publicly delivered prompts/audio can always be copied; authentication/quotas reduce bulk leakage, not guarantee DRM. Offline packs deliberately increase local extractability, so publish only the intended learner subset and never assessment keys/source archives by accident.

## Geographic/provider evidence and limits

All sources below were opened/fetched on 2026-11-04; these are policy/legal research leads, not final legal advice or network measurements.

- [OpenAI supported countries](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/supported-countries): Iran is absent. The page warns that accessing or offering services outside listed territories may lead to suspension. Therefore an Iran-facing OpenAI dependency is not an approved assumption. A UK entity, reseller, remote API proxy, overseas server or second provider does not establish permission for Iranian end users. Require explicit applicable contractual/legal authorization for this exact service before integrating; do not propose circumvention. No OpenAI account/API call or Iranian reachability test was performed.
- [OpenAI API data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data): API data is not used for training by default, but retention is endpoint/configuration dependent. Default abuse monitoring can retain content for up to 30 days; special controls require approval. Responses application state and files have their own rules; transcription differs. Thus “not trained on” cannot be advertised as “not stored” or “private on device.” If ever authorised, record the chosen endpoint, storage parameters, account settings, contract and deletion semantics; do not assume ZDR eligibility.
- [OFAC FAQ 1110](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1110), dated May 2024, explains that §560.540 superseded GL D-2 and contains narrower conditions, including commercial web-hosting restrictions involving entities located in Iran. [OFAC FAQ 853](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/853), updated May 16, 2024, describes e-learning/edtech activities under §560.540 subject to additional criteria. These pages provide a reason to investigate a lawful service design, not blanket permission for Mitooni or its suppliers.
- **Important conflicting-date evidence:** [Federal Register final rule, August 26, 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-26/pdf/2026-17426.pdf) stays §§560.544, 560.550 and 560.554, plus GL F and GL G, effective August 24, 2026. FAQ 853 still discusses GL G. The later rule defeats reliance on the FAQ's GL G summary alone. It does not itself establish that every edtech service is forbidden or that §560.540 permits this one. Current qualified review must map the operating entity, personnel, service/coaching activities, end users, software, counterparties, data locations and supplier chain against applicable rules, including UK and Iranian obligations; that review has not been obtained.

The existing `iran-ielts-market-and-gtm-plan.md:21,94,115–116,164–166` is expressly superseded as an implementation premise: domestic mirroring, bot submissions, offshore contracts and dual supply are unverified options, not approved availability, privacy or legal solutions. No new vendor was selected, contacted or provisioned. Existing BarEx services are the selected reuse starting point, subject to service-specific eligibility and isolation proof. “Works during a shutdown” cannot be promised: local previously downloaded exercises may work; authentication, syncing, coaching and cloud marking require connectivity. Availability on one network/time proves only that observation.

## Evidence gates before a real learner pilot

These gates are proposals for coordinator adoption. None is reported as passed by this desk review.

1. **Authorization matrix:** in an isolated synthetic environment, learner A/B, coach A/B and revoked/anonymous sessions exercise every list/read/create/patch/delete/upload/download route. Expected: no unauthorized disclosure/mutation; server identity always wins.
2. **Upload and browser boundary:** test oversized/truncated/zero-byte/polyglot/non-audio content, MIME mismatch, invalid duration, path-like filenames, orphan upload and exhaustive/range downloads. Expected bounded rejection/processing and no public object. Run stored-XSS payloads through essay, transcript, titles, notes and feedback; verify text rendering, CSP, cookie and CSRF defenses. Confirm mic stops on leaving/sign-out and permission denial never yields a fake speaking score.
3. **Save/submit recovery:** deterministic disconnect at typing, each audio chunk, save acknowledgment, submit acknowledgment and coach booking/message commit; kill/reload browser and reopen a second tab. Reorder/duplicate requests; expire/revoke session mid-retry; fill storage quota; simulate 401/403/409/429/500, clock skew and provider timeout. Expected: exact last locally acknowledged draft/take survives within the disclosed storage policy; server content hashes match; stale writes conflict visibly; one final submission/job/booking/message; no silent loss or false “saved.” Record local/server revisions and checksums, not learner content. Explicitly test the current swallowed `flush()` failure before migrating that code.
4. **Content and processor boundary:** inspect production JS, sourcemaps, public assets and network payloads for answer keys/transcripts/source archives/provider secrets. Test exam/practice result eligibility and answer enumeration limits. Use synthetic prompt injections requesting another learner's work, source text or tools; expected no cross-job access or actionable tool execution. Provider adapter outage/retry must preserve submission state and distinguish queued, failed, human-reviewed and automated results.
5. **Privacy lifecycle:** seed one synthetic learner with essay, multiple takes, upload orphan, transcript, coach thread and failed job; delete it, then test API/object/cache/outbox/job access, log/search visibility and documented backup expiry. Expected linked content unavailable immediately where promised, no late retry recreates it, audit proves completion without retaining payload. Verify account switching on a shared browser reveals none of the prior account's content.
6. **Provider/legal readiness:** named human owner records approved operating/data boundary, current qualified applicability review and written provider eligibility for actual Iranian users/services. Include hosting/auth/communications/marking/telemetry and failover dependencies, contracts, subprocessors, retention, cost limits and termination/export plan. An alternate model needs its own eligibility and scoring validation. No payment assumptions are validated here.
7. **Authorised real-network pilot:** only after gate 6 and Arash's external-test approval, use synthetic fixtures and consenting testers on at least one fixed and two mobile Iranian networks, representative Android Chrome and iOS Safari devices, without relying on a circumvention route. Record date, coarse network/device, all first/third-party hostnames, DNS/TLS/HTTP outcomes, cold start, login/recovery delivery, essential asset load, audio playback/capture/upload and resumed attempt. Repeat at different times and after a controlled disconnect. Proposed initial pass: every critical journey succeeds on each tested combination, no lost acknowledged answer/take, and retry yields one submission; collect latency distributions and choose acceptable budgets before promising performance. A failure blocks the affected claim/flow, not permission to hide the location. No customer content or identifying IPs in the report. A UK laptop test cannot substitute.
