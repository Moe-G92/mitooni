## Outcome and alternatives

The product needs a navigable, teacher-designed syllabus delivered by the AI tutor, with original teaching, practice, transfer, section training, full mock and review material across all four skills. Human coaching adds interpretation and expert assessment. A catalogue of tests alone cannot supply prerequisite language instruction; an endless chat cannot ensure coverage or preserve independent assessment conditions.

Three practical choices were compared:

- A test-bank-first route gives rapid volume and familiarisation, but repeats exposed items and leaves learners without a teach/repair sequence. Use tests as one layer, not the syllabus.
- A fixed level-by-level textbook sequence is inspectable, but routes uneven learners poorly and can force strong learners through redundant teaching. Keep inspectable sequence and prerequisites without one global level lock.
- An objective graph with teacher-authored packs, optional prerequisites and reserved assessment families supports targeted AI teaching and all-skill coverage. It needs explicit availability, evidence and review controls. This is the proposed approach because it extends the accepted runtime contract without inventing an adaptive ability score.

The first set is **representative original material for teacher review**, deliberately narrower than the complete coverage plan. It must contain usable stimuli and teaching sequences, not merely titles, prompts or schema records. First-set acceptance must never be described as complete IELTS syllabus coverage. ## Official specification checked on 2026-11-06

These compact summaries describe the public construct, not authorization to reproduce official questions.

**Listening:** four parts, 40 questions, about 30 minutes; everyday two-person conversation and monologue, then education/training conversation and academic monologue. Audio is ordinarily heard once, with several English accents. Families: multiple choice, matching, plan/map/diagram labels, form/note/table/flow-chart gaps, sentence gaps and short answers. Instruction-specific limits and accepted answer forms matter. [Official Listening specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening). For computer practice, preserve two minutes of final checking; do not append the historical paper transfer allowance. [British Council current Listening format](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/what-is-ielts/how-it-works/test-format/listening).

**Academic Reading:** three sections, 40 questions, 60 minutes, 2,150–2,750 words total. Non-specialist texts include narrative, description and argument. Cover multiple choice; True/False/Not Given; Yes/No/Not Given; matching information, headings, features and sentence endings; sentence gaps; summary/note/table/flow-chart gaps; diagram labels; short answers. Finding paragraph detail differs from finding its main idea; a missing claim differs from a contradicted claim. [Official Academic Reading specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading).

**Academic Writing:** two tasks in 60 minutes. Task 1 describes supplied visual information, with a 150-word minimum and roughly 20-minute recommendation; Task 2 develops a response/argument, minimum 250 words and roughly 40 minutes. Task 2 carries twice Task 1's weight. [Official Academic Writing specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-writing). The public Writing descriptors are marked updated May 2023. Version the English rubric and separately review Persian explanations. [Official Writing descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf).

**Speaking:** recorded examiner interview, 11–14 minutes. Part 1 familiar-topic interview, 4–5 minutes; Part 2 long turn, one-minute preparation, up to two minutes speaking and follow-up, 3–4 minutes overall; Part 3 related abstract discussion, 4–5 minutes. [Official Speaking specification](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking). A response transcript lacks the auditory evidence required for pronunciation and important fluency features. [Official Speaking descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf).

**Scoring:** objective answers earn raw marks; form-specific raw-to-band requirements vary. Writing criteria: Task Achievement for Task 1 / Task Response for Task 2; Coherence and Cohesion; Lexical Resource; Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Criteria are equally weighted within a task. Speaking criteria are Fluency and Coherence; Lexical Resource; Grammatical Range and Accuracy; Pronunciation, equally weighted. [Official scoring guidance](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail). This does not validate Mitooni's original forms, rater judgements or model output.

**2026 delivery change:** the 5 March 2026 announcement says paper-based delivery ends from mid-2026 on market-specific timelines; selected markets add Writing on Paper. The assessed construct remains unchanged. [IELTS announcement](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/updates-to-ielts-test-delivery). Current official delivery guidance distinguishes centre computer tests, selected handwriting options and Academic online testing; acceptance/use and market availability require checking for the learner's intended sitting. [Ways to take IELTS](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/why-choose-ielts/ways-to-take-ielts). Typed computer familiarisation is the first proposed simulation configuration. Speaking must remain an interactive-examiner construct even when rehearsed through a browser. Do not claim exact live-centre UI fidelity or Iranian availability from these global pages.

## Complete syllabus taxonomy to track, not a first-set completion claim

Every row below needs explicit status: `planned`, `draft`, `in_review`, `approved`, `withdrawn`; plus available teaching, practice, fresh transfer and assessment families. A task-type label alone is not coverage. Required coverage has both language objectives and exam-task demands.

### Shared language and study foundations

- Understand English instructions, response limits, timing and tool conditions; navigate/save/recover, type and edit; listen through headphones and make a usable recording.
- Vocabulary: meaning in context, collocation, word families, spelling, paraphrase, register and appropriate precision. Review in new sentences and productive use, not synonym lists alone.
- Grammar: clause meaning, tense/aspect, agreement, countability, reference, comparison, modification, cause/contrast/condition, complex sentence control and punctuation.
- Discourse: main/supporting ideas, organisation, evidence, inference, stance/hedging and source-versus-personal knowledge distinctions.
- Pronunciation/listening link: intelligible sounds, word stress, chunking, rhythm, connected speech, prominence and intonation. Do not impose accent imitation as success.
- Study regulation: set priorities by skill, attempt before feedback, explain evidence, repair, return later, apply to unfamiliar material; preserve accommodations and distinguish them from substantive assistance.

### Listening objective graph

Track details (names/numbers/dates/spelling), main idea, sequence/directions, relationships, speaker identification, changed decisions, distractors, paraphrase, stance, agreement and academic organisation. Cross those with all six official family groups above, every part/context and several accents/voices. Scaffolds may slow/replay an approved excerpt, expose transcript, segment speech or teach vocabulary; independent attempts retain their declared conditions. Long recordings also require sustained attention and recovery after a missed answer. These are proposed instructional objectives derived from the construct, not a proprietary official syllabus.

### Reading objective graph

Track decoding/sentence meaning, scan/locate, skim/gist, detailed meaning, reference/cohesion, paraphrase, distinction between assertion/evidence/inference, writer stance, paragraph role, argument development and answer/instruction checking. Cross with all eleven official family groups, text genres and increasing discourse/lexical demands. A learner should learn when order helps and when matching tasks require broader search; never teach a universal “all answers are sequential” rule. Keep the passage available when practising reading-with-text. A separate retrieval exercise must not quietly replace the exam construct.

### Writing objective graph

Academic Task 1 tracks understanding the source, selecting an overview, grouping key features, accurate comparisons, quantities/units/time, proportion language, process sequence, spatial change and paragraphing. Asset coverage must include line/bar/pie/table, combined displays, process/diagram and map/plan change. This asset taxonomy is an authoring coverage plan; it is not a promise of a fixed official mix.

Task 2 tracks interpreting every demand, clear position, relevant ideas, reasoning, examples, qualification/counterargument, coherent progression, paragraph development, precise vocabulary, sentence control, revision and time management. Author varied requests for opinion/degree of agreement, discussion with position, advantages/disadvantages or evaluation, causes/problems with solutions, and multiple direct questions. These are useful teaching groupings, not a closed official list or memorisable template system. Apply all four criteria to complete responses; mini paragraphs can demonstrate a narrow objective only.

### Speaking objective graph

Track relevant direct response, extending with reasons/examples, narrative sequence, organising a long turn from notes, paraphrasing around gaps, comparison, explanation, speculation, qualification, turn-taking and responding to an unexpected follow-up. Integrate all four criteria and all three parts. Sample complete speech with hesitation, repair and prosody preserved. Fluency is not maximum words per minute; pronunciation is not transcript spelling. Provide different familiar and abstract topics without predicting live questions.

### Test preparation, section training and review

Include interface familiarisation separately from proficiency evidence; supported mini lessons; independent task families; realistic-length section training; complete timed simulations; expert review; actionable revision; and delayed fresh-family rechecks. Keep a visible coverage map for teachers and an ordinary next activity/syllabus for learners. Internal checkpoint labels, source diligence and review verdicts belong in authoring/review records, not marketing copy.

## Representative paths and teaching sequence

Foundation/intermediate/advanced are **authoring demand hypotheses**, not validated CEFR or IELTS bands. A learner can be foundation in writing and advanced in reading. Initial short checks propose a route with uncertainty and learner/teacher choice, never place a whole person from one score.

- **Foundation:** understand instruction → literal meaning/details → short evidence-backed responses → guided sentences/paragraphs or speech chunks → gradually longer work. Offer Persian explanation, English stimulus and optional English explanation; fade translation in independent work. A short accessible text/audio is a preparatory lesson, not an authentic whole section.
- **Intermediate:** paraphrase, distractors, grouping, developed reasons and sustained response → guided task → complete independent task → new topic → section pacing. Preserve prerequisite support without forcing a global restart.
- **Advanced:** offer independent challenge first; tackle nuanced stance, complex argument, data synthesis, qualification and unexpected follow-up; use feedback on actual weak evidence and task completion. Avoid mandatory elementary instruction or ornamental vocabulary goals. Lack of a difficult enough item means ceiling unavailable, not band 9.

Each module uses: **diagnose → teach → practice → transfer → mock → review**. Diagnose samples the intended objective without revealing the later task; teach uses approved anchors and a purposeful learner check; practice requests an attempt, then feedback and repair; transfer is fresh-family independent work; mock draws on a different reserved pool once appropriate; review separates mistakes, language needs and timing, then prescribes repair and later recheck. Mock is a downstream syllabus layer, not a compulsory full test after each short lesson. Repeated retrieval and spacing are design hypotheses grounded in learning research already reviewed in `learning-pathways-20261105.md`; no exact optimal schedule or IELTS effect size is established. A freshly checked vocabulary experiment found spacing advantages on explicit tests without the same semantic-priming advantage, so vocabulary recall cannot stand for fluent transfer. [Nakata & Elgort, published online 3 June 2020; journal 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0267658320927764).

Progression requirements are objective-specific teacher decisions. Store evidence/conditions and available next choices. Do not infer mastery from time in chat, one right response, a universal percentage, or successful revision of the same essay. A teacher may prescribe support/challenge with a reason; assignments and scores remain separately authorized.

## Teacher and independent review acceptance

Before any learner-facing publication:

- A named IELTS domain reviewer checks each objective/stimulus/task/rubric and actual audio/figure; an independent solver checks every objective key and resolves alternative answers. Record exactly what was reviewed, credentials supplied and residual limits; do not invent examiner accreditation.
- Teacher assesses real differences among the three demand branches, novice floor and advanced ceiling; Persian/English explanations preserve the English construct and mixed-direction reading order. Teaching should elicit an attempt and useful repair without becoming answer dumping.
- Productive anchors are blind-reviewed for criterion fit and practical feedback. Numeric band estimates require a separately reviewed policy, reliable rater/adjudication evidence and held-out evaluation; no band labels are inserted merely because descriptors exist.
- Engineering validates private-key/restricted-family boundaries, pinned editions, audio/figure integrity, recovery and no duplicate submission, plus keyboard/assistive operation. Teacher review cannot replace these tests; test pass cannot establish pedagogical validity.

Suggested later validation collects consented, permitted learner evidence with delayed new-family work, subgroup/error analysis, blind productive marking and adjudication.
